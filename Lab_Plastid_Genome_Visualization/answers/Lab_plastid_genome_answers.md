# Lab Plastid Genome Answers

## Visualize Plastid Genome Structure

### 1. What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?

The scientific name of the chosen plant is *Tulipa greigii*. Its plastid genome accession number is **NC_087048.1**.

### 2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?

The total length of the *Tulipa greigii* chloroplast genome is **152,006 base pairs (bp)**.

### 3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.

Yes. The map shows the typical four-part organization of a circular plastid genome:

- **LSC (Large Single-Copy region)** – the largest region, located at the lower portion of the circular map.
- **SSC (Small Single-Copy region)** – the smaller single-copy region, located at the upper portion of the map.
- **IRa (Inverted Repeat A)** – located between the SSC and LSC on the right side.
- **IRb (Inverted Repeat B)** – located between the SSC and LSC on the left side.

The two inverted repeat regions are approximately opposite each other and separate the LSC and SSC regions.

### 4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.

Three examples of genes visible in the LSC region are:

- **rpoA** – encodes a subunit of the plastid RNA polymerase.
- **petD** – encodes a component of the cytochrome b6/f complex involved in photosynthetic electron transport.
- **psbA** – encodes the D1 protein of photosystem II.

These genes are distributed within the large single-copy portion of the genome.

### 5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.

One example is **ndhF**, which is located in the SSC region. The ndhF gene is associated with the chloroplast NADH dehydrogenase-like complex and participates in photosynthetic electron transport.

### 6. Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?

One example is **rpl2**, which occurs within the inverted repeat region. Because the plastid genome contains two highly similar inverted repeat regions, genes located within the IRs can occur in two copies, with one copy in each IR.

Therefore, the duplicated occurrence is associated with the duplicated IR regions.

### 7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.

The **psbA** gene is a photosynthesis-related gene visible on the map. It encodes the D1 protein of photosystem II. The D1 protein is an important component of the photosynthetic reaction center and is involved in the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis.

### 8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?

The arrows indicate the orientation of the genes and the direction in which they are transcribed. Genes located on opposite DNA strands can therefore be transcribed in opposite directions. The arrows help show the arrangement and orientation of genes around the circular plastid genome.

### 9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.

The GC content does not appear completely uniform across the genome. The graph shows fluctuations in GC content around different regions of the plastid genome. Some portions show relatively higher or lower GC content than others. This indicates regional variation in base composition, but the graph alone does not establish the biological cause of these differences.

### 10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?

A graphical plastid genome map makes the organization of the genome easier to understand. It allows the LSC, SSC, and inverted repeat regions to be seen immediately, while also showing the locations, orientations, and functional groups of genes around the circular genome. It is easier to visualize gene distribution and genome structure from the map than from a long DNA sequence or a list of annotations.
